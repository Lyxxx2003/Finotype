Module1: Paycheck Literacy

Submodule1: The Fiscal Ship: 
link: https://fiscalship.org/
Summary: The Fiscal Ship is an educational interactive simulation designed by the Brookings Institution and the Hutchins Center to help users understand the complexities of the U.S. federal budget. Participants are tasked with selecting specific policy priorities—such as strengthening national defense, reducing inequality, or promoting low taxes—while simultaneously implementing fiscal measures to put the federal debt on a sustainable downward trajectory. By forcing players to make difficult trade-offs between competing social and economic goals, the tool vividly illustrates the long-term challenges of fiscal policy and the mathematical reality of balancing domestic spending with debt reduction targets.

Submodule2: The Uber Game: 
link: https://ig.ft.com/uber-game/
Summary: The Uber Game is an award-winning interactive news feature by  the Financial Times that places players in the role of a full-time Uber driver. Based on real-world reporting and data from drivers in San Francisco, the simulation challenges players to navigate the daily pressures of the "gig economy"—balancing fuel costs, vehicle maintenance, and customer ratings while striving to earn $1,000 for a mortgage payment. By forcing participants to make high-stakes decisions under time constraints and fluctuating incentives, the game vividly illustrates the precarious nature of platform labor and the thin margins of modern independent contracting.

Submodule3: Everything Deducted From Your Paycheck, Explained (Earnest)
link: https://www.earnest.com/blog/paycheck-deductions
Summary: Earnest’s guide to paycheck deductions provides a comprehensive breakdown of the various factors that reduce an employee's gross pay to their final take-home amount. The article categorizes these deductions into three main types: mandatory government taxes (such as FICA and federal income tax), voluntary pre-tax benefits (including 401(k) contributions and health insurance premiums), and post-tax deductions like life insurance or union dues. By explaining how these adjustments impact taxable income and long-term financial health, the piece empowers workers to better navigate their paystubs, optimize their retirement savings, and understand the true value of their total compensation package beyond the base salary.

Submodule4: A Paycheck Overview for You and Your Child (Benjamin Talks)
link: https://www.benjamintalks.com/thevault/decoding-a-paycheck
Summary: Benjamin Talks: Decoding a Paycheck serves as an essential financial literacy guide designed to demystify the complex components of a modern paystub. The article breaks down the critical distinction between gross and net pay, explaining how mandatory tax withholdings like FICA and federal income tax impact a worker's bottom line. Additionally, it clarifies the role of voluntary deductions, such as employer-sponsored retirement plans and health insurance premiums. By simplifying these financial terms, the piece aims to empower individuals—especially those new to the workforce—to accurately track their earnings, understand their tax obligations, and take control of their personal budgeting and savings goals.。

Module3: Spending Control
Description: Managing necessities, flexible spending, and budgeting habits
Submodule1: How to Stress Less About Money: 4 Simple Rules | YNAB
link: https://www.ynab.com/blog/ynab-four-rules-less-stress
Summary: YNAB’s Four Rules framework offers a proactive philosophy designed to transform a user's relationship with money from reactive stress to intentional control. The rules—Give Every Dollar a Job, Embrace Your True Expenses, Roll with the Punches, and Age Your Money—work together to break the paycheck-to-paycheck cycle. By treating infrequent costs as monthly obligations and allowing for flexible adjustments when life changes, this method encourages users to spend only what they have already earned. Ultimately, the system aims to create a financial "buffer," providing the peace of mind that comes from knowing exactly how every cent contributes to long-term stability and personal priorities.

Submodule2: The 50/30/20 Rule: A Guide to Budgeting Your Money (Investopedia)
link: https://www.investopedia.com/ask/answers/022916/what-502030-budget-rule.asp
Summary: Investopedia’s guide to the 50/30/20 rule outlines a straightforward mathematical framework for personal wealth management, popularized by Senator Elizabeth Warren. The rule suggests allocating 50% of after-tax income to "needs" (such as housing and groceries), 30% to "wants" (including dining out and hobbies), and the remaining 20% to financial goals, such as debt repayment or retirement savings. By simplifying the budgeting process into three broad categories, this method helps individuals maintain a sustainable lifestyle while ensuring consistent progress toward long-term stability. It is particularly effective for those who find granular expense tracking overwhelming but still seek a disciplined approach to their monthly cash flow.

Submodule3: The "30-Day Rule" for Impulse Purchases (The Balance)
link: https://www.listenmoneymatters.com/what-is-the-30-day-rule/
Summary: The Balance Money’s guide to the 30-day rule introduces a simple yet powerful psychological tactic to curb impulsive spending and regain financial discipline. The rule suggests that when you feel the urge to buy a non-essential item, you should wait exactly 30 days before completing the purchase. During this cooling-off period, the initial emotional "high" of shopping dissipates, allowing for a more rational assessment of whether the item is a true necessity or a fleeting want. By redirecting that potential expenditure into a savings account instead, consumers can build a significant financial buffer while ensuring that their spending aligns with their long-term values rather than momentary impulses.

Module4: Credit & Debt
Description: Using credit cards safely and understanding borrowing costs

Submodule1: The Evolution of Trust
link: https://ncase.me/trust/
Summary: The Evolution of Trust is a profound interactive game by Nicky Case that utilizes game theory to deconstruct the mechanics of social cooperation and systemic credit. Through a series of simulated "Trust Games," users experience how individual choices—whether to "Cooperate" or "Cheat"—impact long-term collective outcomes. The simulation illustrates that while cheating may offer short-term gains, persistent honesty and the "Tit-for-Tat" strategy foster the most resilient environments. By highlighting the roles of repeat interactions, possible miscommunications, and win-win incentives, the project reveals that trust is not just a moral choice, but a functional evolution necessary for a stable society.

Submodule2: Credit Card Interest Calculator (Visualized)
link: https://www.nerdwallet.com/credit-cards/learn/credit-card-interest-calculator
Summary: NerdWallet’s Credit Card Interest Calculator is a powerful financial tool designed to visualize the long-term cost of carrying a balance. By allowing users to input their current debt, interest rate (APR), and monthly payment amount, the calculator generates a clear timeline for debt freedom. Most importantly, it calculates the "interest penalty"—the total extra money paid to the bank beyond the original purchase price. By highlighting the dramatic difference between paying the "minimum" versus a fixed monthly amount, the tool uses real-time data to encourage disciplined repayment and reveals the true mathematical weight of high-interest credit card debt.

Submodule3: The Psychology of Credit Card Spending (Psychology Today)
link: https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/the-science-behind-behavior/201607/does-it-matter-whether-you-pay-cash-or-credit-card
Summary: The Psychology of Today’s "Does It Matter Whether You Pay Cash or Credit Card?" explores the cognitive phenomenon known as the "Pain of Paying." The article explains that paying with physical cash triggers an immediate emotional sting, acting as a natural brake on spending. In contrast, credit cards decouple the pleasure of a purchase from the pain of payment, creating a "psychological distance" that leads to higher expenditure and less memorable spending. By examining how different payment transparently impacts our brain's valuation of products, the piece warns that moving toward a cashless society may inadvertently weaken our self-control and distort our perception of financial value.

Submodule4: Confessions of a Credit Card Representative (Reader's Digest)
link: https://www.rd.com/list/times-to-never-use-your-credit-card/
Summary: Reader’s Digest’s guide to strategic credit card use identifies specific financial scenarios where swiping plastic can lead to long-term debt or unnecessary risk. The article warns against using credit for "cash-equivalent" transactions—such as lottery tickets, wire transfers, or casino chips—which often trigger immediate high-interest cash advance fees. It also advises caution when dealing with struggling businesses or making small, impulsive purchases that can lead to "balance creep." By highlighting the danger of using credit for "bridge spending" during a financial crisis, the piece emphasizes that a credit card should be a tool for planned expenses rather than a high-interest substitute for an emergency fund.

Submodule5: Credit Scores Explained with Pizza (Visual Capitalist)
link: https://www.visualcapitalist.com/visualizing-the-evolution-of-consumer-credit/
Summary: Visual Capitalist’s infographic on the evolution of consumer credit provides a sweeping historical perspective on how borrowing has transformed from ancient clay tablets to modern digital scores. The visual timeline highlights key milestones, such as the mid-20th century birth of the Diners Club card and the subsequent nationalization of credit reporting standards. By illustrating the shift from character-based lending in small communities to algorithmic risk assessment via FICO scores, the piece demystifies the data-driven nature of modern finance. This visualization underscores how credit has evolved from a localized social trust into a global, instantaneous infrastructure that defines individual purchasing power and economic mobility in the 21st century.
